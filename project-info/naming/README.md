The name Daedalus Thread comes from the mythological figure Daedalus, who was a skilled craftsman and inventor in Greek mythology. The term "Thread" refers both to the Digital Thread concept in engineering and the idea of a continuous line of development or connection. Together, the name symbolizes innovation, flight, and the seamless integration of technology and design, reflecting the project's focus on advanced engineering and continuous improvement.

The project is structured into 5 phases, each representing a key stage in the development and implementation of the Daedalus Thread system. These phases are thematically named to reflect the progression from initial concept to final deployment:

1. DT-1 Icarus: Icarus was the first human to fly using wings crafted by Daedalus. This phase represents the initial concept and design of the Daedalus Thread system, where a COTS airframe is fitted with custom wings integrated through a digital thread. 

2. DT-2 Perdix: Perdix was the Daedalus's brilliant young nephew and apprentice who invented the compass and mechanical saw. When cast down from Acropolis, Athena caught him and transformed him into a partridge. This phase represents the fully-formed aircraft, forged through the digital thread, flying low, safe, and with predictable stability.

3. DT-3 Talos: Talos was the lengendary bronze giant who protected Crete from invaders. This phase represents the transition from a manually flown aircraft to an autonomous robot capable of performing complex missions, with the digital thread enabling advanced control.

4. DT-4 Argus: Argus Panoptes was the hundred-eyed gaurdian who never closed all his eyes at once. This phase represents real-time visibility, closing the loop between the aircraft and real-time decision making through a ground station. 

5. DT-5 Proteus: Proteus was the primortial sea god capable of changing his shape at will to adapt to any circumstance. This phase represents the ability of the aircraft to adapt to changing mission requirements, different payloads, and onboard intelligence.
